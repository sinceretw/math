5-Week Calculus Readiness Plan

For a rising 11th-grader at Whitney Young — finished Pre-Calc in 10th grade, starting Calculus after summer

How to use this plan

You have ~3 hours/day for 5 weeks (~105 hours). The plan front-loads Pre-Calc review (Weeks 1–2) so that the Calculus material (Weeks 3–5) sits on a solid foundation. Calculus rarely fails because of "the calculus" — it fails because of weak algebra, factoring, and trig. So the review weeks matter as much as the new material.

Daily rhythm (3 hours):

  • 0:00–0:20 — Warm-up: redo 2–3 problems you missed the day before (spaced repetition).
  • 0:20–1:20 — Learn / review the day's concept + work the examples by hand.
  • 1:20–1:30 — Break.
  • 1:30–2:40 — Do the exercise set. Write every step. No mental shortcuts.
  • 2:40–3:00 — Check answers, mark every mistake, and write a one-line note on why you missed it.

Rules that actually make this work:

  1. No calculator unless a problem says so. Calculus exams are mostly no-calculator.
  2. When you check answers, the goal is not "how many right" — it's "find every misunderstanding."
  3. Keep a running "mistake log" notebook. Review it every Sunday.
  4. If a concept feels shaky, slow down — it's fine to spend an extra day. The schedule has slack built in.

Answer keys are at the end of each concept. Try the full set before peeking.

PART 1 — PRE-CALCULUS REVIEW (Weeks 1–2)

These are the five pre-calc pillars calculus leans on hardest. Each has a concept refresher, a worked example, and an exercise set (≥5 problems) with answers.

Concept 1 — Functions: domain, range, composition, inverses, transformations

Key ideas

  • Domain = allowed inputs. Watch for (a) denominators ≠ 0, (b) even roots of negatives, (c) logs of non-positives.
  • Composition: $(f\circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$ — inner function first.
  • Inverse: swap $x$ and $y$, solve for $y$. $f^{-1}$ undoes $f$: $f(f^{-1}(x)) = x$. A function has an inverse only if it's one-to-one (passes the horizontal line test).
  • Transformations of $y=f(x)$:
  • $f(x)+k$ up $k$; $f(x)-k$ down $k$
  • $f(x-h)$ right $h$; $f(x+h)$ left $h$
  • $a\,f(x)$ vertical stretch by $a$; $f(bx)$ horizontal compress by $b$
  • $-f(x)$ flip over $x$-axis; $f(-x)$ flip over $y$-axis

Worked example. If $f(x)=\sqrt{x-3}$, find the domain and $f^{-1}(x)$. Domain: need $x-3\ge 0 \Rightarrow x\ge 3$. Inverse: let $y=\sqrt{x-3}$, swap → $x=\sqrt{y-3}$, square → $x^2=y-3$, so $f^{-1}(x)=x^2+3$ for $x\ge 0$.

Exercises 1

  1. Find the domain of $f(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{x^2-4}$.
  2. Find the domain of $g(x)=\sqrt{5-2x}$.
  3. Given $f(x)=x^2+1$ and $g(x)=2x-3$, find $(f\circ g)(x)$ and $(g\circ f)(x)$.
  4. Find the inverse of $f(x)=\dfrac{2x-1}{x+4}$.
  5. Describe the transformations turning $y=x^2$ into $y=-2(x+3)^2-5$, in order.
  6. If $f(x)=|x|$, sketch (describe) $y = f(x-2)+1$.
  7. Is $f(x)=x^3-x$ one-to-one on all reals? Explain.

Answers 1: (1) $x\ne \pm 2$, i.e. $(-\infty,-2)\cup(-2,2)\cup(2,\infty)$. (2) $x\le \tfrac52$. (3) $(f\circ g)(x)=(2x-3)^2+1=4x^2-12x+10$; $(g\circ f)(x)=2(x^2+1)-3=2x^2-1$. (4) $f^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{-4x-1}{x-2}$. (5) Left 3, vertical stretch ×2, reflect over $x$-axis, down 5. (6) V-shape with vertex at $(2,1)$. (7) No — it fails the horizontal line test (e.g., $f(0)=f(1)=0$); not one-to-one.

Concept 2 — Polynomial functions: factoring, zeros, end behavior

Key ideas

  • Factoring toolkit: GCF → difference of squares $a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)$ → trinomials → grouping → sum/difference of cubes $a^3\pm b^3=(a\pm b)(a^2\mp ab+b^2)$.
  • Zeros / roots: set $=0$ and factor. A root $r$ with multiplicity → graph touches (even mult.) or crosses (odd mult.) at $x=r$.
  • End behavior depends only on the leading term $a_nx^n$:
  • $n$ even, $a_n>0$: both ends up. Even, $a_n<0$: both down.
  • $n$ odd, $a_n>0$: down-left, up-right. Odd, $a_n<0$: up-left, down-right.
  • Factoring is the #1 calculus survival skill — limits, derivatives set to zero, and integration all hinge on it.

Worked example. Factor completely: $x^3-2x^2-9x+18$. Group: $x^2(x-2)-9(x-2)=(x-2)(x^2-9)=(x-2)(x-3)(x+3)$. Zeros: $2,3,-3$.

Exercises 2

  1. Factor: $6x^2-x-12$.
  2. Factor completely: $x^3-27$.
  3. Factor completely: $2x^3+10x^2-2x-10$.
  4. Find all real zeros and their multiplicities: $f(x)=x^2(x-1)^3(x+4)$.
  5. State the end behavior of $f(x)=-3x^4+2x^2-7$.
  6. Factor: $x^4-16$.
  7. A cubic crosses the $x$-axis at $x=-1$ and $x=2$, and touches at $x=0$. Write a possible factored form.

Answers 2: (1) $(3x+4)(2x-3)$. (2) $(x-3)(x^2+3x+9)$. (3) $2(x+5)(x-1)(x+1)$. (4) $x=0$ (mult 2), $x=1$ (mult 3), $x=-4$ (mult 1). (5) Both ends down. (6) $(x-2)(x+2)(x^2+4)$. (7) $f(x)=a\,x^2(x+1)(x-2)$ for some $a\ne0$.

Concept 3 — Rational functions: asymptotes, holes, simplifying

Key ideas

  • Vertical asymptote where the reduced denominator $=0$.
  • Hole where a factor cancels from numerator and denominator.
  • Horizontal asymptote by comparing degrees of numerator ($n$) and denominator ($d$):
  • $nd$: no HA (slant if $n=d+1$).
  • Complex fractions — multiply top and bottom by the common denominator. This exact skill appears constantly in derivative-definition limits.

Worked example. For $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2-x-2}$: factor → $\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x-2)(x+1)}=\dfrac{x-1}{x-2}$. Hole at $x=-1$, vertical asymptote $x=2$, horizontal asymptote $y=1$ (equal degrees).

Exercises 3

  1. Find all vertical asymptotes and holes of $f(x)=\dfrac{x-3}{x^2-9}$.
  2. Find the horizontal asymptote of $g(x)=\dfrac{3x^2+1}{x^2-4}$.
  3. Find the horizontal asymptote of $h(x)=\dfrac{2x+5}{x^3-1}$.
  4. Simplify the complex fraction $\dfrac{\frac1x-\frac1{3}}{x-3}$.
  5. Simplify $\dfrac{\frac{1}{x+h}-\frac1x}{h}$ (this is exactly a derivative setup — keep it).
  6. Find the slant asymptote of $\dfrac{x^2+1}{x}$.

Answers 3: (1) VA at $x=-3$, hole at $x=3$. (2) $y=3$. (3) $y=0$. (4) $\dfrac{-1}{3x}$ (since $\frac1x-\frac13=\frac{3-x}{3x}=\frac{-(x-3)}{3x}$). (5) $\dfrac{-1}{x(x+h)}$. (6) $y=x$.

Concept 4 — Exponentials & logarithms

Key ideas

  • $\log_b(xy)=\log_b x+\log_b y$, $\;\log_b\frac{x}{y}=\log_b x-\log_b y$, $\;\log_b(x^k)=k\log_b x$.
  • $b^{\log_b x}=x$ and $\log_b(b^x)=x$ (inverses).
  • Natural base $e\approx 2.718$; $\ln x=\log_e x$. Calculus lives in base $e$ — get comfortable now.
  • Change of base: $\log_b x=\dfrac{\ln x}{\ln b}$.

Worked example. Solve $3^{2x-1}=27$. Write $27=3^3$, so $2x-1=3$, $x=2$.

Exercises 4

  1. Solve $2^{x+1}=32$.
  2. Solve $\ln(x)+\ln(x-3)=\ln 10$.
  3. Condense to one log: $2\ln x-\tfrac12\ln y+\ln 3$.
  4. Expand: $\log\!\left(\dfrac{x^3\sqrt{y}}{z^2}\right)$.
  5. Solve $5\,e^{0.2x}=20$.
  6. Solve $\log_2(x)+\log_2(x-2)=3$.
  7. Evaluate without a calculator: $\log_3 81$, $\;\ln(e^4)$, $\;e^{\ln 7}$.

Answers 4: (1) $x=4$. (2) $x=5$ (reject $x=-2$, not in domain). (3) $\ln\!\dfrac{3x^2}{\sqrt y}$. (4) $3\log x+\tfrac12\log y-2\log z$. (5) $x=5\ln 4\approx 6.93$. (6) $x=4$ (reject $x=-2$). (7) $4,\;4,\;7$.

Concept 5 — Trigonometry: unit circle, identities, equations, graphs

Key ideas

  • Unit circle — memorize $\sin,\cos$ at $0,\tfrac\pi6,\tfrac\pi4,\tfrac\pi3,\tfrac\pi2$ and their reflections. This is non-negotiable for calculus.
  • Pythagorean identity: $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$; dividing gives $1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta$.
  • $\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. Reciprocals: $\csc,\sec,\cot$.
  • Double angle: $\sin 2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta$, $\;\cos 2\theta=\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta=1-2\sin^2\theta$.
  • Graphs: for $y=A\sin(Bx+C)+D$ — amplitude $|A|$, period $\tfrac{2\pi}{B}$, phase shift $-\tfrac{C}{B}$, midline $y=D$.

Worked example. Solve $2\sin\theta-1=0$ on $[0,2\pi)$. $\sin\theta=\tfrac12 \Rightarrow \theta=\tfrac\pi6,\tfrac{5\pi}6$.

Exercises 5

  1. Give exact values: $\sin\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$, $\cos\tfrac{5\pi}{4}$, $\tan\tfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\sin\tfrac{7\pi}{6}$.
  2. Simplify $\dfrac{\sin^2\theta}{1-\cos\theta}$ (hint: use $\sin^2=1-\cos^2$).
  3. Solve $2\cos^2\theta-1=0$ on $[0,2\pi)$.
  4. Solve $\sin 2\theta=\sin\theta$ on $[0,2\pi)$.
  5. For $y=3\sin\!\left(2x-\tfrac\pi2\right)+1$: state amplitude, period, phase shift, midline.
  6. Verify the identity $\dfrac{1-\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta}=\sin\theta$.
  7. If $\cos\theta=\tfrac35$ and $\theta$ is in quadrant IV, find $\sin\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.

Answers 5: (1) $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\,-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,\sqrt3,\,-\tfrac12$. (2) $1+\cos\theta$. (3) $\theta=\tfrac\pi4,\tfrac{3\pi}4,\tfrac{5\pi}4,\tfrac{7\pi}4$. (4) $2\sin\theta\cos\theta=\sin\theta\Rightarrow \sin\theta(2\cos\theta-1)=0\Rightarrow \theta=0,\pi,\tfrac\pi3,\tfrac{5\pi}3$. (5) Amp 3, period $\pi$, phase shift $+\tfrac\pi4$, midline $y=1$. (6) LHS $=\tfrac{\sin^2\theta}{\sin\theta}=\sin\theta$. ✓ (7) $\sin\theta=-\tfrac45$, $\tan\theta=-\tfrac43$.

PART 2 — CALCULUS (Weeks 3–5)

Five core concepts, each with a refresher, a worked example, and ≥10 questions with answers. Work them without a calculator unless noted.

Concept 6 — Limits

Key ideas

  • A limit $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L$ asks: what value does $f$ approach as $x$ nears $a$? (The actual value $f(a)$ doesn't matter.)
  • Direct substitution works when $f$ is continuous at $a$. Try it first.
  • If you get $\tfrac00$ (indeterminate), factor and cancel, or rationalize, or simplify a complex fraction, then substitute.
  • One-sided limits: $\lim_{x\to a^-}$ (from the left) and $\lim_{x\to a^+}$ (from the right). The two-sided limit exists only if both sides agree.
  • Limits at infinity: divide every term by the highest power of $x$ in the denominator. (Same rules as horizontal asymptotes.)
  • Special limit: $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1$.

Worked example. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}$. Sub gives $\tfrac00$. Factor: $\tfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}=x+2\to 4$.

Exercises 6

  4. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sqrt{x+9}-3}{x}$
  5. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{3x^2-2x+1}{5x^2+7}$

  7. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{2x^3-x}{x^2+5}$

  9. $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^+}\frac{1}{x}$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^-}\frac{1}{x}$ — do they agree?
  10. For $f(x)=\begin{cases}x+1,& x<2\\ x^2,& x\ge 2\end{cases}$, find $\lim_{x\to 2^-}f(x)$, $\lim_{x\to 2^+}f(x)$, and state whether $\lim_{x\to2}f(x)$ exists.

Answers 6: (1) $-4$. (2) $-2$. (3) $4$ (rationalize or factor $x-4=(\sqrt x-2)(\sqrt x+2)$). (4) $\tfrac16$. (5) $\tfrac35$. (6) $0$. (7) $\infty$ (diverges). (8) $3$. Concept 7 — Continuity

Key ideas

  • $f$ is continuous at $a$ if all three hold: (i) $f(a)$ is defined, (ii) $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ exists, (iii) $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)$.
  • Types of discontinuity: removable (a hole — limit exists but ≠ value, or value undefined), jump (one-sided limits disagree), infinite (vertical asymptote).
  • Polynomials are continuous everywhere. Rational/trig/log are continuous on their domains.
  • Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT): if $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $N$ is between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, then $f(c)=N$ for some $c$ in $(a,b)$. Used to prove a root exists.

Worked example. Find $k$ so that $f(x)=\begin{cases}x^2,& x\le 1\\ kx+1,& x>1\end{cases}$ is continuous. Need left limit $=$ right limit: $1=k+1\Rightarrow k=0$.

  6. State where $f(x)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x-4}}$ is continuous.

  8. Use the IVT to show $\cos x = x$ has a solution in $(0,1)$.
  9. Is $f(x)=\tan x$ continuous at $x=\tfrac\pi2$? Explain.
  10. For $f(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\sin x}{x},& x\ne 0\\ 1,& x=0\end{cases}$, is $f$ continuous at $0$? Justify.

(10) Yes — $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1=f(0)$. (11) e.g. $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-25}{x-5}$.

Concept 8 — The derivative (limit definition)

Key ideas

  • Definition: $\displaystyle f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$.
  • Meaning: instantaneous rate of change = slope of the tangent line at a point.
  • Alternate form (slope at a specific point $a$): $\displaystyle f'(a)=\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a}$.
  • Tangent line at $x=a$: $y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)$.
  • The complex-fraction and rationalizing skills from Week 2 are exactly what these limits require.

Worked example. Use the definition for $f(x)=x^2$. $\dfrac{(x+h)^2-x^2}{h}=\dfrac{2xh+h^2}{h}=2x+h\to 2x$. So $f'(x)=2x$.

Concept 9 — Differentiation rules

Key ideas

  • Power rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}$. Constant multiple and sum rules apply termwise.
  • Product rule: $(fg)'=f'g+fg'$.
  • Quotient rule: $\left(\dfrac fg\right)'=\dfrac{f'g-fg'}{g^2}$.
  • Chain rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}f(g(x))=f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)$ — "derivative of outside (leaving inside alone) × derivative of inside."
  • Key derivatives: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin x=\cos x$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos x=-\sin x$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x=\dfrac1x$.

Worked example (chain). $\dfrac{d}{dx}(3x^2+1)^5 = 5(3x^2+1)^4\cdot 6x = 30x(3x^2+1)^4.$

Concept 10 — Applications of derivatives + intro to integration

Key ideas — applications

  • Increasing/decreasing: $f'>0$ increasing, $f'<0$ decreasing.
  • Critical points: where $f'=0$ or undefined. First derivative test classifies local max/min by sign change of $f'$.
  • Concavity: $f''>0$ concave up, $f''<0$ concave down; inflection where $f''$ changes sign.
  • Optimization: model the quantity, take the derivative, set $=0$, verify it's the max/min.
  • Related rates: differentiate a relationship with respect to time $t$ (chain rule everywhere).

Key ideas — integration (intro)

  • Antiderivative: $F$ with $F'=f$. Power rule reversed: $\displaystyle\int x^n\,dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C$ ($n\ne -1$).
  • Fundamental Theorem of Calculus: $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)$ where $F'=f$.
  • A definite integral = signed area under the curve.

Worked example (optimization). A farmer has 100 ft of fence for a rectangular pen against a wall (3 sides fenced). Maximize area. Let width $x$, length $100-2x$. $A(x)=x(100-2x)=100x-2x^2$. $A'(x)=100-4x=0\Rightarrow x=25$. Area $=25\cdot50=1250$ ft².

  13. Find the area under $f(x)=2x$ from $x=0$ to $x=3$ (check against the triangle area).

Answers 10: (1) $f'=3x^2-6x=3x(x-2)$; $x=0$ local max ($f=1$), $x=2$ local min ($f=-3$). (2) $f'=3x^2-12>0\Rightarrow x<-2$ or $x>2$. (3) $f''=6x$; concave down $x<0$, up $x>0$; inflection at $(0,0)$. (4) $f'=2x-4=0\Rightarrow x=2$; check endpoints: $f(0)=1,f(2)=-3,f(3)=-2$ ⇒ max $1$ at $x=0$, min $-3$ at $x=2$. (5) $w=3$ (a $3\times3$ square, area 9). (6) $10$ and $10$ (product 100). (7) $\tfrac{dV}{dt}=4\pi r^2\tfrac{dr}{dt}=4\pi(25)(2)=200\pi\approx 628$ cm³/s. (8) $x^3-2x^2+5x+C$. (9) $\tfrac23x^{3/2}-x^{-1}+C$. (10) $\tfrac83$. (11) $\big[2\sqrt x\big]_1^4=4-2=2$. (12) $\big[-\cos x\big]_0^\pi=1-(-1)=2$. (13) $\big[x^2\big]_0^3=9$ ✓ (triangle: $\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot6=9$).
